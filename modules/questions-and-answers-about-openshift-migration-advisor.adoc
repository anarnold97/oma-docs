= Questions and answers about the {OMA}

[role="_abstract"]
These topics build on xref:modules/overview-of-openshift-migration-advisor.adoc#overview-of-openshift-migration-advisor[Overview of the {OMA}], xref:modules/how-you-access-oma.adoc#how-you-access-oma[How you use the OpenShift migration advisor in the Hybrid Cloud Console], and xref:modules/audience-and-scale-for-oma.adoc#audience-and-scale-for-oma[Who typically uses the service, and at what scale]. The sections that follow cover comparisons, scope, timing, report contents, demos, release cadence, language availability, and very large VMware estates.

== Does the {OMA} cost extra beyond my existing agreements?

No. The advisor itself is a no-cost, self-service tool in typical offerings. Console availability and labels still follow your {RH} entitlement or program; navigation details stay in xref:modules/how-you-access-oma.adoc#how-you-access-oma[How you use the OpenShift migration advisor in the Hybrid Cloud Console].

== Which hypervisors does the {OMA} document here?

This guide covers VMware vSphere only. Other hypervisors are out of scope.

== How does the {OMA} compare to VMA?

VMA is a {RH} consulting engagement with workshops and architects for large or complex migration programs. The {OMA} is an automated report in the {HCC}. Teams often run the {OMA} first for a baseline, then use VMA or other programs to operationalize.

== How does the {OMA} compare to RVTools?

RVTools exports vSphere inventory to a spreadsheet. The {OMA} consumes an RVTools export in a supported format or agent-collected inventory and applies rules and analytics to produce migration-oriented views in an automated migration report.

== Where in our program should we run the {OMA}?

During discovery and evaluation—before you finalize designs or execution—when administrators and architects want inventory-backed signals without committing to a full migration plan yet. See also xref:modules/problems-that-an-automated-migration-report-is-meant-to-help-with.adoc#problems-that-an-automated-migration-report-is-meant-to-help-with[Problems that an automated migration report is meant to help with].

== What appears in an automated migration report?

See xref:assemblies/understanding-the-automated-report.adoc#understanding-the-automated-report[Understanding the automated migration report] for risks, readiness views, and outputs. At a high level: discovery-style inventory, warnings or risks the rules surface, and recommendations or estimates your build exposes.

[id="release-cadence-oma"]
== How often the {OMA} changes

The {OMA} is developed as a web service on the {HCC}. Ship cadence mixes larger increments with frequent updates to the live experience.

The {OMA} is continuously updated as a web service. Larger features are typically delivered on a monthly sprint cadence. You should also expect smaller features and fixes to appear on a weekly cadence as the service is updated.

[id="language-support-oma"]
== Language availability for the product and this guide

Today, the {OMA} user interface and this guide are available in English. Future releases might broaden regional or language coverage. Any change will be announced through normal product communication channels.

[id="support-for-large-environments-oma"]
== Using the {OMA} in very large VMware estates

The user experience is tuned for common midsize footprints. Larger organizations can still use the service for an early view, within product limits and your own policies.

The {OMA} is often used by organizations whose VMware footprint is in a midsize range on average, including environments on the order of two thousand virtual machines. That focus does not block you from uploading inventory from a larger estate. If you operate at very large scale, you can still use the {OMA} for initial investigation and automated reporting, and you should confirm limits, support expectations, and operational approach with your {RH} account team or partner where applicable.
